# A Strategic Freight Market Model for Liner Shipping: Pricing, Entry, and Endogenous Service Choice

**Authors**: Jonas Westin
**Date**: 2026-11-10
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2611.14169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14169)

## Summary

This paper introduces a strategic freight market model for liner shipping that treats prices, market entry, and service characteristics as endogenous outcomes of strategic operator interaction. The framework evaluates oligopolistic competition, monopoly, Ramsey pricing, and welfare-maximising planning under logit demand and shared cost structures. Using a solution method based on global pricing best responses and analytical profit bounds, a Baltic-North Sea application demonstrates how market organization and fuel shocks shape service provision, prices, and welfare.

## Key Contributions

- Formulates a strategic freight market model for liner shipping where prices, entry, and service provision are endogenous outcomes of operator interaction.
- Develops a solution method computing global pricing best responses and evaluating unilateral service deviations using analytical profit bounds.
- Demonstrates through a Baltic-North Sea application that system-wide Ramsey pricing captures most welfare-maximising planner gains without deficits, whereas tight substitutes can lead competition to mimic monopoly outcomes.

## Limitations

The application is synthetic and meant to illustrate qualitative mechanisms rather than forecast actual markets.
