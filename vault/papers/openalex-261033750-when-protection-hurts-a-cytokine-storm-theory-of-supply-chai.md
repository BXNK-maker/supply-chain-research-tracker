# When Protection Hurts: A Cytokine Storm Theory of Supply Chain Overreaction

**Authors**: Gurkirat Wadhwa
**Date**: 2026-10-24
**Paper ID**: [openalex:2610.33750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33750)

## Summary

This paper develops a decision-theoretic framework to analyze how defensive supply chain actions, such as hoarding and duplicate ordering, can sometimes amplify congestion and worsen disruptions. The author introduces Response-Induced Loss (RIL) and derives a closed-form critical response threshold—termed a Supply Chain Cytokine Storm—beyond which defensive measures become more harmful than the initial shock. Furthermore, a spectral stability condition is established for interconnected supply networks to determine when decentralized responses remain bounded or propagate uncontrollably.

## Key Contributions

- Developed a decision-theoretic framework modeling Protective Response Intensity (PRI) and Response-Induced Loss (RIL) in supply chains.
- Derived a closed-form critical response threshold (Supply Chain Cytokine Storm) beyond which self-induced defensive costs exceed the original disruption.
- Established a spectral stability condition for interconnected supply networks to distinguish bounded decentralized responses from network-wide propagation.
