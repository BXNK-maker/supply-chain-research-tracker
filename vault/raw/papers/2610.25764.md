Abstract
Time-series models increasingly condition on known future inputs.
In controlled systems, some of these inputs are actions that can be
changed, compared, and optimized before execution.
A model must therefore do more than predict the realized future:
it must predict how outcomes change under different actions from the
same observed history and support reliable comparison of candidate plans.
We introduce
ActTime
, an action-conditioned world model for time series.
Over the observed history,
ActTime
updates variable-specific latent states
through an action-conditioned transition and corrects them using observations.
It then rolls the same transition forward under candidate actions to generate
future trajectories.
Training uses only factual trajectories, without paired alternative-action
outcomes or planning supervision.
We construct a simulator-grounded evaluation suite across building energy,
traffic, greenhouse climate, and inventory management, covering factual
prediction, intervention-effect prediction, offline decision making, and
closed-loop planning.
ActTime
ranks first in 12 of 16 domain-task settings.
Across eight methods, the mean pairwise model-ranking reversal rate
is 17.8% between factual prediction and intervention fidelity,
and rises to 28.4% between intervention fidelity and decision ranking.
These results show that factual accuracy, intervention fidelity,
and decision quality are related but not interchangeable.

Introduction
Modern time-series forecasting increasingly incorporates information beyond the
target history. Models use multivariate observations, static attributes,
exogenous covariates, and known-future inputs
(
Lim et al., 2021
;
Das et al., 2023
;
Wang et al., 2024
;
Li et al., 2026
;
Han et al., 2026
)
.
These advances broaden forecasting from history-only extrapolation to
conditional prediction with rich contextual information
(
Yamaguchi et al., 2026
;
Meyer et al., 2026
)
.
In controlled systems, however, some future inputs serve as decision variables
rather than fixed context: they are actions selected by a decision maker and
can be changed before execution, compared against alternatives, and optimized
for downstream objectives.
A forecasting model can in principle condition on an action sequence, but
decision making requires more than predicting the future under one prescribed
sequence.
Given the same observed history, the model must predict how future outcomes
vary across candidate action sequences, rather than only the outcome under
the realized sequence, since these relative differences determine which
actions should be preferred before execution.

Factual evaluation assesses the future generated by the action sequence that
was actually executed, as in Fig.

.
Accurate prediction of this trajectory does not directly test whether changing
the future actions produces the correct change in the resulting trajectory.
This gives the first gap:
factual accuracy does not guarantee intervention fidelity
.
A model can fit the realized future well while still misestimating the effect of an
alternative action sequence, as illustrated in Fig.

.
A similar failure mode has been observed in recent action-conditioned world
models, where performance on demonstrated actions can mask incorrect responses
to valid alternative actions
(
Wang, 2026
;
Chen et al., 2026
)
.
The second gap appears when these predictions are used to choose actions.
A planner compares candidate action sequences according to their predicted
outcomes, so the decision depends on their
relative ordering
, not only
on the accuracy of each individual response.
Prediction errors can therefore reverse the ordering of candidate actions even
when their trajectory-level errors appear moderate
(
Donti et al., 2017
;
Elmachtoub and Grigas, 2022
)
.
Thus,
intervention fidelity does not by itself guarantee decision
quality
, as illustrated in Fig.

.
Related work on world-model evaluation has similarly moved beyond predictive
or visual fidelity toward direct assessment of policy rankings and closed-loop
task performance
(
Quevedo et al., 2026
;
Zhang et al., 2026
;
Yu et al., 2026
)
.
Our experiments quantify both gaps across four controlled domains and eight
methods: the average pairwise method-ordering reversal rate is

These gaps call for a model that represents controlled dynamics and can be

This is the central role of a world model: learning action-conditioned dynamics

While this view is well established in model-based control and embodied agents,
related ideas have also been explored in action-conditioned numerical dynamics
and, more recently, in controlled and interventional time-series modeling
(
Shaj et al., 2021
;
Shaj Kumar et al., 2023
;
Wang, 2026
;
Meng et al., 2026
;
Wu et al., 2026
)
.
Yet a factual trajectory reveals only the action sequence that was executed and
the future that followed it; matched outcomes under alternative actions are not
observed.
This leaves a practical question:
can a time-series model learn
action-conditioned dynamics from factual trajectories that remain
reliable under alternative actions for intervention-effect prediction and decision
making
?
We address this question with
ActTime
, an action-conditioned time-series
world model built around a shared controlled transition.
Over the observed history, variable-specific latent states are propagated under
the executed actions and corrected using each new observation, allowing the
transition to model action-conditioned evolution while the latent state remains
grounded in the realized trajectory.
At the history boundary, a lightweight residual prior from a frozen pretrained
temporal backbone refines the terminal state.
The same transition is then rolled forward under candidate future actions
without access to future observations.
Alternative-action predictions therefore arise directly from the action-conditioned dynamics learned on factual trajectories.
To evaluate this capability, we construct simulator-grounded evaluation suites
across four controlled systems.
Training uses only factual trajectories, while same-history interventions,
candidate-action comparisons, and closed-loop planning episodes are generated
only for evaluation.
This provides direct ground truth for the progression in Fig.
1
without paired alternative-action outcomes or planning supervision during training.
Our main contributions are threefold:
•
Decision-oriented formulation and evaluation gaps.
We distinguish factual accuracy, intervention fidelity, and decision
quality as three distinct evaluation criteria for action-conditioned
time-series models, and show that model performance does not reliably
transfer across adjacent criteria.
•
Action-conditioned time-series world model.
We introduce
ActTime
, which learns variable-specific latent states with
a shared action-conditioned transition and observation-based correction.
The same transition is used for observed-history modeling and open-loop
rollout under candidate future actions, using factual supervision only.
•
Simulator-grounded evaluation suite.
We construct evaluation suites across four heterogeneous domains that cover
factual prediction, same-history intervention fidelity, offline decision making, and closed-loop planning.
The underlying simulators provide reference action effects and decision
outcomes, enabling direct evaluation of whether action-conditioned models
remain reliable when used for decision making.

Related Work
Time-Series Forecasting with Covariates
Time-series forecasting has long incorporated information beyond target history, including static attributes, exogenous variables, and known-future inputs
(
Wen et al., 2017
;
Rangapuram et al., 2018
;
Salinas et al., 2020
)
. NBEATSx, TFT, and TiDE integrate such covariates through dedicated components or feature selection
(
Olivares et al., 2023
;
Lim et al., 2021
;
Das et al., 2023
)
. More recent models focus on interactions between endogenous and exogenous series, including TimeXer, ExoTST, CrossLinear, and GCGNet
(
Wang et al., 2024
;
Tayal et al., 2024
;
Zhou et al., 2025b
;
Li et al., 2026
)
. Covariate-aware forecasting has also reached TSFMs
(
Yamaguchi et al., 2026
)
. Chronos-2 supports multivariate and covariate-informed forecasting
(
Ansari et al., 2025
)
, while UniCA adapts pretrained TSFMs to heterogeneous covariates
(
Han et al., 2026
)
. These models show that contextual information can improve forecasting, but an action is not merely given context. It is changed, compared, and optimized. Factual accuracy therefore provides limited evidence about responses to alternative actions.
Action-Conditioned Time-Series Modeling
Several recent studies explicitly model actions or interventions in temporal systems. ThermoForce separates passive dynamics from control-induced responses and combines a frozen TSFM prior with a structured intervention model for HVAC control
(
Wang, 2026
)
. CEDAR learns action-conditioned demand dynamics for counterfactual simulation and budget planning
(
Meng et al., 2026
)
. DRIFT and NormWear-2 model physiological trajectories under treatment interventions
(
Liu et al., 2026
;
Luo et al., 2026
)
, while DoFlow unifies observational, interventional, and counterfactual time-series prediction in a causal generative framework
(
Wu et al., 2026
)
. Related work in longitudinal causal inference estimates outcomes under alternative treatment sequences using recurrent, representation-learning, and Transformer-based models
(
Lim, 2018
;
Bica et al., 2020
;
Melnychuk et al., 2022
;
Xiong et al., 2024
)
. These methods mainly target domain-specific intervention modeling or causal treatment-effect estimation. We instead study a common action-conditioned modeling framework across heterogeneous controlled systems and evaluate whether predictive accuracy translates into intervention fidelity and decision quality.
World Models for Decision Making
World models learn dynamics that can be rolled forward under candidate actions for planning and policy learning
(
Ma et al., 2024
;
Zhou et al., 2025a
)
. PlaNet and Dreamer use latent imagined trajectories for decision making
(
Hafner et al., 2019
;
Hafner et al., 2020
;
Hafner et al., 2025
)
, while TD-MPC2 combines latent dynamics with trajectory optimization
(
Hansen et al., 2024
)
. AcRKN and MTS3 model action-conditioned transitions in numerical dynamical systems, including long-horizon prediction under control inputs
(
Shaj et al., 2021
;
Shaj Kumar et al., 2023
)
. Another line studies reusable dynamics priors. APV pretrains from action-free videos before learning action-conditioned dynamics for control
(
Seo et al., 2022
)
, with related ideas explored in recent world-model pretraining
(
Gao et al., 2025
;
Wan et al., 2026
)
. Evaluation has also moved beyond predictive fidelity. WorldGym tests whether simulated rollouts preserve policy performance and rankings
(
Quevedo et al., 2026
)
, World-In-World evaluates closed-loop task success
(
Zhang et al., 2026
)
, and recent work examines fidelity under alternative actions
(
Chen et al., 2026
)
. We follow this decision-oriented view and evaluate factual prediction, intervention fidelity, offline decision making, and closed-loop planning.

The rank changes in Table

We compare adjacent evaluation levels on matched held-out histories.
Prediction measures reference-trajectory error, Effect measures the fidelity of
action-induced trajectory changes, and Decision measures candidate-action
ranking quality.
For each method pair, we record whether its relative ordering reverses between
adjacent levels.
Using orderings rather than raw metric differences makes the comparison
insensitive to the different scales of the three criteria.

5
Conclusion
We study action-conditioned time-series modeling from the perspective of
world modeling for decision making, where the goal is not only to predict the
realized future but also to reason about outcomes under alternative actions.
We introduce
ActTime
, which learns action-conditioned dynamics over variable-specific latent states from factual trajectories and rolls the same dynamics forward under candidate future actions.
This formulation supports factual forecasting, intervention-effect prediction,
and downstream planning without requiring paired alternative-action outcomes
or planning supervision.
Across four simulator-grounded controlled systems,
ActTime
performs strongly
throughout this evaluation pipeline, while our analysis shows that factual
accuracy, intervention fidelity, and decision quality are related but not
interchangeable.
These findings motivate treating alternative-action reasoning and downstream
decision quality as first-class objectives when developing and evaluating
action-conditioned time-series models.

A.2
Evaluation Protocols and Metrics
We evaluate all methods in four settings: factual prediction,
intervention-effect prediction, offline decision making, and closed-loop
planning.
For intervention and offline evaluation, all methods use the same held-out
histories, candidate action banks, and simulator-generated outcomes.
For each held-out history, all candidate action sequences are rolled out from the identical simulator state at the history boundary. Stochastic simulator components and exogenous realizations are matched across candidates, so differences in future trajectories arise only from the candidate actions.
Alternative-action outcomes are used only for evaluation and never for
training or model selection.
Within each domain, closed-loop evaluation uses the same simulator episodes,
candidate-generation protocol, and CEM budget across learned methods.

A.3
Implementation Details
Model configuration.
We use the same architecture and shared hyperparameters across all four
domains, with environment-specific input dimensions and temporal horizons.
Each observed variable is represented by a 144-dimensional latent state.
The controlled dynamics use two transition blocks with four-head
self-attention across variables and a SwiGLU update.
The observation encoder and action router both incorporate first differences,
as described in Section
3.1
.
The prediction head outputs the
0.1
0.1
,
0.5
0.5
, and
0.9
0.9
quantiles, with the
median used for point prediction.
Temporal prior.
We use a frozen six-layer EIDOS backbone with hidden dimension 384 and
12 attention heads.
Only the target histories are passed to EIDOS.
Each target is standardized over its observed history, encoded independently, and passed through the same frozen projection to 128 dimensions.
The projected representations are averaged across targets, and the final
history representation is passed through the residual adapter in
Section
3.2
.
The adapter gate is initialized to zero.
The EIDOS backbone remains frozen throughout training, and the temporal prior
is injected only at the history boundary.
Training and model selection.
Stage A trains the controlled core and prediction head for 10 epochs using
Adam with learning rate
10
−
3
10^{-3}
and batch size 256.
Stage B freezes the Stage-A predictor and the EIDOS backbone and trains only
the 60,321-parameter history-prior adapter for 3 epochs using AdamW with
learning rate

and weight decay

Both stages optimize the mean pinball loss
(

Final checkpoints are selected solely by factual validation MAE.
No intervention-effect or planning metric is used for model selection.
A.4
Baseline Adaptations
All baselines are trained on the same factual data splits as
ActTime
.
For intervention and offline evaluation, they use the same held-out histories,
candidate action banks, and simulator outcomes.
Historical actions are provided together with the observed history, and future
actions are introduced causally so that prediction at horizon
h
h
never
depends on actions beyond that horizon.
Within each domain, closed-loop evaluation uses the same simulator episodes,
candidate-generation protocol, and CEM budget across learned methods.
Baseline checkpoints are selected using factual validation MAE only.
Forecasting baselines.
We adapt Ridge-VARX, DLinear, PatchTST, iTransformer, and TFT to condition on
future actions while retaining their native forecasting backbones.
Ridge-VARX performs direct multi-horizon regression using recent observations,
historical actions, and the future-action prefix available at each horizon.
Its lag length and ridge coefficient are selected on factual validation data.
For DLinear, PatchTST, and iTransformer, we attach a causal
action-conditioning head to the corresponding time-series encoder.
The representation for each target and forecast horizon is combined with the
historical actions and the available prefix of candidate future actions before
prediction.
DLinear operates on target histories after trend-seasonal decomposition,
PatchTST uses channel-independent temporal representations, and iTransformer
allows information exchange across observation variables before action
conditioning.
TFT follows its native known-future-input formulation.
Historical observations and actions are processed by the historical encoder,
while candidate future actions are supplied to the decoder as known-future
inputs.
Its median quantile is used as the point prediction.
World-model baselines.
RSSM and MTS3 are evaluated as action-conditioned latent dynamics models.
RSSM infers its latent state from the observed history through
action-conditioned transitions and observation updates, then rolls the latent
prior forward under candidate future actions.
MTS3 similarly filters the observed history into structured latent beliefs and
propagates them under future actions at its fast and slow temporal scales.
For both models, future observations are unavailable during evaluation, and
target trajectories are decoded from the action-conditioned latent rollout.

Appendix C
Limitations
Our evaluation is simulator-grounded.
This is intentional, as evaluating alternative action sequences from the same
history requires access to outcomes that are generally unavailable in
observational time-series data.
The four environments span building energy, traffic control, greenhouse
climate, and inventory management, but performance in these simulators does
not by itself establish transfer to real-world systems.
An important next step is evaluation on real-world controlled-intervention
datasets or deployment settings in which responses to selected actions can
be observed prospectively.
The evaluation therefore measures predictive fidelity under controlled
interventions rather than causal identification from observational data
with unobserved confounding.
ActTime
is trained exclusively on factual trajectories, without paired
outcomes for alternative actions.
Consequently, learning how outcomes change with action relies on variation
present across the training trajectories.
We use multiple behavior policies and structured control snippets to
broaden this coverage, and evaluate within the feasible action ranges of each
environment.
The present experiments do not address extrapolation to substantially
different action regimes that are unsupported by the training data.
Characterizing uncertainty under such distribution shifts and adapting the
dynamics with limited new interaction are useful directions for future work.
Finally, our planning experiments use finite-horizon MPC with CEM-based
action-sequence optimization.
This setting isolates whether the learned dynamics are accurate enough to
compare candidate actions and support closed-loop decisions, but does not
address substantially longer-horizon planning or hierarchical control.
In addition, although
ActTime
predicts multiple quantiles, the current
planner primarily uses point trajectory predictions when comparing candidate
plans.
Using predictive uncertainty explicitly for risk-sensitive planning is a
natural extension.
