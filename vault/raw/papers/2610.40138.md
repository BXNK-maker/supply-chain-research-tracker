Abstract.
In recent years, a growing number of online retail companies have embraced deep learning models for demand forecasting, leveraging their scalability in processing large-scale datasets. Accurate demand forecasts enable improved planning and allow operational flexibility to adjust sales accordingly. Moreover, quantile estimation has gained widespread application as it offers valuable uncertainty quantification, thereby enhancing the robustness of decision-making processes. However, limited research has addressed the effective quantification of uncertainty when target labels are ambiguous. In retail, actual sales can be capped by stock and boosted by temporary promotions, whereas the target is underlying demand. Such scenarios are characterized by double censoring and no deep-learning pipeline yet exists for such data. In this paper, we introduce a straightforward framework for learning quantiles from doubly-censored observations and propose a tailored evaluation metric. Our approach is supported by theoretical guarantees, and we empirically demonstrate that our metric correlates well with the oracle calibration measure. Experimental results show that our estimator surpasses existing baselines. Finally, inventory simulations and real-world applications confirm the practical utility of our method in retail operations.

1.
Introduction
In supply chain management, quantile regression is highly valued for its distribution-free nature and ability to estimate uncertainty. Retail giants like JD and Amazon utilize deep learning-based quantile estimation for inventory optimization
(
Qi et al., 2023
;
Park et al., 2022
;
Petropoulos et al., 2022
)
. However, when sales data are censored, such as due to stockouts or promotions, quantile regression estimates can become biased. As illustrated in
Figure
1
, right-censoring happens with perishable items, where low stock causes frequent stockouts and conceals true demand. On the flip side, left-censoring occurs with promotional products, where temporary promotions lead to observed sales surpassing actual demand. Daily necessities typically have sufficient stock and rarely go on promotion, so their sales data usually reflects the actual demand. When right and left censoring coexist in one dataset, it’s called doubly censoring.
Figure 1.
Censored data example.
Using or discarding doubly censored data both distort forecasts: the former fits the model on biased labels, the latter discards usable information
(
Zhong et al., 2021
)
. In practice, retailers often revise sales using ad hoc business rules, yet without true demand they cannot verify the accuracy or feed the revised data back into the forecasting pipeline. Consequently, demand forecasting still lacks an end-to-end training method for doubly censored data and an appropriate metric to evaluate models under this scenario. Survival analysis offers a direct parallel: only a lower bound on lifetime is observed, producing right-censored responses. Re-weighting techniques developed for this setting motivate our unified framework, which trains any deep network end-to-end with the Doubly Portnoy Estimator (DPE). Likewise, while quantile forecasts are usually judged by calibration and sharpness, no calibration test exists for doubly censored data. Borrowing again from survival methodology, we propose Extended D-Calibration to assess the calibration of competing models under double censoring.
In conclusion, this paper presents a unified framework for training and evaluating deep models under doubly censored data. Although motivated by e-commerce demand forecasting, the framework is domain-agnostic and can be extended to any setting with similar censoring patterns. Our contributions are as follows.
•
We introduce an end-to-end algorithm that trains deep models with the DPE, and provide the first formal proof of its properness under doubly censored data, closing a long-standing theoretical gap in quantile estimation with incomplete observations.
•
To evaluate models trained under such censoring, we propose Extended D-Calibration, a new metric for doubly censored data, and prove that it precisely gauges the gap between modelled and true probabilities under mild assumptions.
•
We first validate the proposed metric and algorithm through simulations, then confirm their effectiveness on two large-scale real-world datasets. Inventory simulations further demonstrate their superiority in stock control, and online A/B tests verify their practical impact.
2.
Related Work
2.1.
Probabilistic Demand Forecasting
Probabilistic demand forecasting plays a crucial role in inventory management across various sectors. A widely studied problem in this domain is the newsvendor problem, which identifies a specific quantile of the demand distribution as the optimal inventory level
(
Ban and Rudin, 2019
;
Beutel and Minner, 2012
)
. Traditional approaches for estimating demand quantiles typically rely on deterministic forecasts and the historical residuals of predicted values
(
Levi et al., 2015
)
. With the rise of deep learning, neural network-based demand forecasting has become increasingly prevalent. For example, to capture uncertainty, DeepAR employs a Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) to generate hidden states and estimates distribution parameters through maximum likelihood estimation
(
Salinas et al., 2020
)
. This approach requires assumptions about the underlying data distribution.
In contrast, quantile regression offers a non-parametric approach for probabilistic forecasting that does not require distributional assumptions
(
Koenker and Bassett Jr, 1978
)
. Building on this foundation, Wen, Lim, and other researchers
(
Wen et al., 2017
;
Lim et al., 2021
)
have designed Seq2Seq models that directly estimate quantiles using quantile loss as the loss function. However, these models typically provide predictions for a fixed set of quantiles. To enable arbitrary quantile forecasting, a spline-based neural network have been proposed
(
Gasthaus et al., 2019
;
Park et al., 2022
;
Taieb, 2022
;
Brando et al., 2022
)
. These networks leverage the flexibility of splines to interpolate between quantiles, thereby allowing for more granular probabilistic forecasts.
In the theoretical analysis of probabilistic forecasting, the theory of scoring rules serves as a fundamental framework. A loss function that qualifies as a proper scoring rule ensures that model training with this function can elicit the true underlying distribution
(
Gneiting and Raftery, 2007
)
. To evaluate the effectiveness of different proper scoring rules, recent studies have widely adopted calibration and sharpness as key evaluation criteria
(
Chung et al., 2021
;
Taieb, 2022
;
Feldman et al., 2021
)
. Our study also employs these metrics to evaluate model performance.
2.2.
Censored Quantile Regression
As highlighted in the introduction, censored data remain largely overlooked in demand forecasting. To exploit such data, we draw upon survival analysis, a field with an extensive literature on censoring (see
(
Wang et al., 2019
;
Peng, 2021
)
for a comprehensive survey).
Deep learning has recently been combined with survival analysis under right censoring
(
Zhong et al., 2021
;
Katzman et al., 2018
)
.
Some studies use censored data during training
(
Avati et al., 2020
;
Goldstein et al., 2020
)
. In the statistical literature, doubly censored data have been studied via quantile regression
(
Lin et al., 2012
;
Ji et al., 2012
;
Peng, 2021
)
, but few works link censored quantile regression to deep models.
The
q-rnn
package trains networks with left censoring
(
Cannon, 2018
)
.
Pearce et al. extend Portnoy’s Estimator to train deep models via an iterative re-weighting algorithm
(
Pearce et al., 2022
)
.
Yanagisawa shows that Portnoy’s Estimator is proper, so the true distribution is recovered by optimization
(
Yanagisawa, 2023
)
.
On the evaluation side, the C-index
(
Harrell et al., 1982
)
quantifies median discrimination only, whereas D-Calibration
(
Haider et al., 2020
)
assesses calibration under right censoring. Neither extends to doubly censored data. We close this gap by introducing Extended D-Calibration, the first metric to evaluate calibration in the presence of both left and right censoring.

Empirical results on synthetic data confirm that Extended D-Calibration aligns most closely with the oracle calibration score. On the two real-world datasets, where true demand is unavailable for censored observations, we rank models solely by Extended D-Calibration and sharpness. As shown in

5.4.3.
Inventory Online A/B Test
For the A/B test, we randomly assign products from multiple warehouses, across different subcategories, to treatment and control groups with equal probability. Given the trade-off between overstocking rate and stockout SKU ratio, we collect data on both metrics over a 7-day period. The comparison shows that, for a fixed stockout SKU ratio, our model reduces the overstock rate by 8% compared to the incumbent online algorithm, demonstrating its practical value in production environments.
6.
Conclusion
This study develops a novel probabilistic framework that explicitly addresses doubly censored data prevalent in e-commerce demand forecasting. Specifically, we propose a deep learning-based quantile regression framework, design the Doubly Portnoy Estimator (DPE), an iterative re-weighting algorithm for training deep models with censored data, and develop Extended D-Calibration, a metric to evaluate models under double censoring. The efficacy of our framework is demonstrated via simulation and two real-world datasets. Application results verify that the proposed method effectively mitigates overstocking and stockout losses in inventory management, thereby improving operational efficiency and profitability.
