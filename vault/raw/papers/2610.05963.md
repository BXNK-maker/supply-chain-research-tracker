Abstract
Smart ports increasingly depend on IoT-enabled digitalization to turn heterogeneous operational processes into measurable, optimizable workflows. Accordingly, this paper first provides a concise review of IoT-enabler technologies for port digital transformation, emphasizing how sensing, connectivity, and data integration capabilities support real-time decision-making in port environments. Motivated by the growing emphasis on operational decarbonization reflected in the European Green Deal and the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), we then present a data-driven fleet scheduling framework for landside port operations at the Port of HaminaKotka (Mussalo terminal), Finland. The scheduling framework reallocates vehicle operating hours to minimize total CO
2
eq emissions while preserving the same aggregate workload. The study uses a two-year dataset from 77 port-operating vehicles, analyzed at monthly and annual aggregation levels, to derive vehicle-specific intensity metrics (CO
2
eq per operating hour) and identify reallocation opportunities. We formulate the scheduling task as a linear programming workload allocation problem that minimizes the weighted sum of assigned hours while keeping total required hours fixed, enforcing fleet-level caps on CO
2
and energy use, and respecting per-vehicle operating limits. The optimization is solved using the HiGHS linear programming solver via SciPy’s linprog. Results show that shifting utilization away from high-intensity vehicles and toward more efficient units yields substantial system-level improvements with no reduction in total operating hours: the optimized schedule achieves an approximately 31% reduction in total CO
2
eq and a simultaneous 9.4% reduction in total energy consumption.

Introduction
Maritime transport remains the backbone of international trade—moving over 80% of goods traded worldwide by volume—which makes ports critical system nodes whose operational efficiency has direct implications for energy use and environmental performance
[
1
]
. Alongside the broader push for sustainable industry—reflected in initiatives such as the European Green Deal and the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)—there is growing pressure to reduce the carbon footprint of maritime transport and port‑related operations without compromising throughput. Recent evidence shows that emissions from global shipping remain substantial and have continued to rise in the post‑pandemic period: the OECD’s AIS‑based estimates indicate that global maritime transport CO
2
emissions increased from 889 Mt (2019) to 973 Mt (2024) (+9.4%), with a marked acceleration in 2024
[
2
]
. Independent inventory work similarly reports large and persistent shipping emissions through 2016–2023, reinforcing the urgency of operational measures that can cut emissions in the near term
[
3
]
. Because ports are repeatedly identified as strategic intervention points for decarbonisation—through operational efficiency, electrification, and smarter energy use—optimization of day‑to‑day logistics decisions (e.g., fleet scheduling and utilization) becomes a practical lever for measurable emissions reduction within existing infrastructure constraints.
To act on these decarbonisation pressures in a measurable and operationally feasible way, ports increasingly rely on Internet of Things (IoT)–enabled digitalization to transform physical processes into data streams that can be monitored, analysed, and optimized in near real time
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
. Smart-port research emphasizes that IoT infrastructures provide the visibility layer needed to coordinate complex, multi-actor terminal operations (e.g., equipment status, asset condition, and logistics flows), thereby enabling data-driven decisions that improve efficiency and sustainability outcomes
[
7
]
. Recent literature reviews further highlight digitalization as a defining characteristic of smart ports, supporting automation, energy management systems (EMS), and performance improvement by integrating sensing, connectivity, and data platforms into operational workflows
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
. From an energy-and-emissions perspective, seaport decarbonisation studies similarly identify digital technologies as key enablers because they strengthen situational awareness and make operational optimisation actions—such as scheduling, resource allocation, and energy-aware planning—implementable at scale
[
11
]
.
In the above context, the digital visibility enabled by smart-port IoT infrastructures, the present work leverages operational data a real-world case study at the Port of HaminaKotka (Mussalo terminal) to demonstrate a scheduling approach that targets CO
2
eq reduction in landside fleet operations. Landside vehicle fleets represent a controllable component of port operations, and prior studies have highlighted fleet scheduling and resource allocation as promising levers for improving efficiency and environmental performance in logistics and industrial systems
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
.
Using a two-year dataset from 77 port-operating vehicles, we formulate the scheduling task as a linear programming–based workload allocation problem that reallocates operating hours to minimize total CO
2
eq emissions while maintaining fixed operational demand and respecting feasible operating limits. Linear optimization has been widely adopted for large-scale scheduling and energy-aware resource allocation due to its transparency, computational efficiency, and suitability for decision support in industrial environments
[
16
]
. By grounding the formulation in operational port data, the proposed approach demonstrates how IoT-enabled digital processes can be coupled with optimization to deliver practical, near-term decarbonization of port logistics without requiring changes to infrastructure or service levels. The key contributions of the work are summarized below.
•
The paper provides an overview of IoT-enabled port digitalization with optimization by showing how operational data generated through digitalized port processes can be transformed into actionable scheduling decisions for emissions reduction in landside logistics.
•
A linear programming–based workload allocation model is developed and applied to a real-world case study at the Port of HaminaKotka (Mussalo terminal) using a two-year dataset from 77 vehicles, minimizing total CO
2
eq emissions while preserving total workload and respecting fleet-level and per-vehicle constraints.
•
The proposed scheduling approach achieves an approximately 31% reduction in total CO
2
eq emissions and a 9.4% reduction in energy consumption solely through schedule reallocation, demonstrating a practical, near-term decarbonization lever that requires no infrastructure changes.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows: Section II provides key enabling technologies for an IoT-enabled framework, Section III formulates the fleet scheduling problem, Section IV explains the results and discussion, and finally, Section V provides the conclusion of the paper.

Advanced Communication and Edge–Cloud Computing Infrastructure

6
Conclusion
A linear programming framework was developed and applied to allocate vehicle work hours in a manner that minimizes CO
2
eq emissions while honoring fixed total demand and energy limits. By solving a compact LP with the HiGHS solver, the method efficiently reassigns workload toward the most efficient vehicles and away from high‑emission units. The case results demonstrate that significant environmental benefits can be achieved without curtailing operations; in this study, total CO
2
eq dropped by 31.26% , accompanied by 12.64% reduction in total energy use. The vehicle‑level shift analysis further clarifies the mechanism of improvement: high‑intensity vehicles experience sharp reductions in hours, whereas low‑intensity vehicles take on additional utilization to satisfy demand. These findings indicate that optimization‑based scheduling is an effective, explainable tool for fleet decarbonization. Future work should integrate operational realism through minimum/maximum utilization constraints, vehicle availability and maintenance windows, and multi‑objective formulations that jointly optimize emissions, energy, and cost.
