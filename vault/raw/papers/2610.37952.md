Abstract
Rounded capacity cuts (RCCs) are among the most effective cuts for capacitated vehicle-routing relaxations, but exact separation is computationally prohibitive at scale. Existing heuristic and neural separators can be fast, but often fail to produce sufficiently many effective cuts within practical separation budgets. We introduce SAG-Sep, a separator that scores node membership in RCC-inducing subsets across vehicle-count levels from a fractional LP solution in a single encoder forward pass, avoiding the iterative predict-and-coarsen inference used by NeuralSEP-style separators. SAG-Sep encodes the LP solution as a Sparse Augmented Graph (SAG), adding flow-informed multi-hop edges that expose long-range routing structure without densifying attention. Beyond LP-aware sparse encoding, we observe that high-value exact RCCs are typically nested, organizing into onion-like subset families, and SAG-Sep’s node scores track subset nesting depth. This motivates Onion-Sweep (OS), a training-free decoder that ranks customers by their scores, evaluates the RCC violation of every prefix of the ranking in one vectorized pass, and emits the most violated prefix of each replica together with a cross-replica family of nested proposals, converting independent node scores into structurally coordinated RCC candidates without a tuned threshold or probing band. On the NeuralSEP benchmark with
N
=
1000
N=1000
customer instances, SAG-Sep with OS reduces the average root gap by 17.7% relative to the strongest NeuralSEP baseline, while the single-pass SAG-Sep separator achieves a roughly 30-fold per-iteration neural separation speedup. Our findings suggest that LP-aware sparse encoding and the nested structure of high-value cuts are structural patterns worth leveraging in neural separation more broadly.

Introduction
The capacitated vehicle routing problem (CVRP) is a canonical NP-hard combinatorial optimization problem and a central testbed for large-scale
routing algorithms
(
Uchoa et al., 2017
)
. State-of-the-art exact
solvers are built on branch-and-cut (B&C) or branch-price-and-cut (BPC)
frameworks
(
Lysgaard et al., 2004
;
Pessoa et al., 2020
;
You and Yang, 2025
)
, where
cutting planes iteratively tighten linear relaxations to improve dual bounds. Among the many valid inequalities proposed for CVRP,
rounded capacity cuts
(RCCs) are particularly effective and are routinely used in both B&C and BPC solvers
(
Lysgaard et al., 2004
;
Fukasawa et al., 2006
)
.
Despite their effectiveness, RCCs pose a fundamental scalability challenge. Separating violated RCCs is NP-hard
(
Diarrassouba, 2017
)
, and exact separation formulations rapidly become computationally prohibitive as the number of customers grows
(
Augerat et al., 1995
;
Fukasawa et al., 2006
;
Pavlikov et al., 2024
)
. Although exact methods provide strong per-iteration tightening of the linear program (LP) relaxation, they are impractical at large scales, forcing solvers to rely on heuristics. The widely used CVRPSEP heuristic
(
Lysgaard, 2003
)
performs well on small-to-medium instances, but on larger problems it often enters a low-throughput regime, generating few effective cuts and delivering only marginal improvements in the dual bound.
Recent work has explored learning-based alternatives. NeuralSEP
(
Kim et al., 2024
)
introduced the first neural RCC separator by training a graph neural network (GNN) to predict cut-defining customer subsets from fractional LP solutions. However, operating solely on the sparse LP-support graph limits scalability: fixed-depth message passing captures only local structure, while deeper networks suffer from over-smoothing and over-squashing
(
Li et al., 2018
;
Alon and Yahav, 2020
;
Wu et al., 2021
)
. NeuralSEP addresses this with recursive graph coarsening, but this multi-stage pipeline incurs substantial training and inference overhead, restricts GPU parallelism, and requires repeated CPU–GPU synchronization. These limitations motivate a separator that captures long-range dependencies without sequential coarsening and instead enables highly parallel, hardware-efficient computation.
To address these challenges, we propose
SAG-Sep
, a single-pass RCC separator built around a
Sparse Augmented Graph
(SAG) representation of the current LP solution. SAG starts from the LP-support graph and augments it with a small number of flow-informed multi-hop edges, exposing long-range routing structure while retaining sparse attention. Given this representation, SAG-Sep scores customer membership in RCC-inducing subsets across vehicle-count levels in one encoder pass, avoiding the iterative predict-and-coarsen procedure of NeuralSEP-style separators.
The continuous vehicle-count conditioning also creates a richer test-time search problem. Aggressive probing of the frozen network showed that it contains useful candidate cuts beyond the hard-threshold subset of each replica, but such probing treats the replicas as independent predictions. Studying the exact RCC separator revealed a consistent pattern: high-value exact cuts often form nested
onion
families, providing cut diversity through ordered subset growth rather than unrelated threshold decisions. This observation leads to
Onion-Sweep (OS)
, a training-free decoder that reads subsets directly off the score ranking instead of thresholding it: within each replica, OS evaluates the RCC violation of every prefix of the score ranking in one vectorized pass and emits the most violated one; across replicas, it aggregates the scores into a global priority ordering and emits the nested prefixes at which the required vehicle count increases. No tuned threshold, band width, or sampling is involved, and the trained encoder is unchanged.
1.1
Contributions
We summarize our contributions as follows:
•
We introduce
SAG-Sep
, a one-shot RCC separator based on a
Sparse Augmented Graph
encoding of the fractional LP solution. The SAG representation preserves the sparsity of the LP-support graph while adding flow-informed multi-hop edges, enabling the model to capture long-range routing dependencies without dense attention or NeuralSEP-style iterative coarsening. This design substantially improves GPU efficiency and sharply reduces neural separation time while retaining strong root-node bounds.
•
We study test-time enhancement in the learned probability space: aggressive conditioned probing, which densifies the vehicle-count conditioning grid, improves root bounds and reveals that the trained network contains useful cuts beyond those exposed by hard-thresholded replicas.
•
We further analyze the learned score space together with exact RCC separation and identify a stable nested, onion-like structure among high-value exact cuts. This motivates
Onion-Sweep (OS)
, a training-free decoder that replaces hard thresholding by a violation-guided sweep over the score ranking within each replica and by onion-guided nested proposals across replicas, removing the threshold dependence of independently thresholded outputs and substantially improving separation effectiveness.
•
More broadly, the design illustrates how a neural module can take over an NP-hard separation subroutine at the root node of an exact solver, accelerating relaxation tightening without sacrificing the validity of generated cuts.

A complementary direction incorporates learning directly into
exact
solvers, leveraging solver states such as LP solutions and dual prices within B&C or BPC frameworks while preserving optimality guarantees
(
Bengio et al., 2021
)
. A major line of work focuses on
learning to branch

;

. Learning has also been explored for

)
, whereas learning-based

(

)
.

6
Conclusion
We introduced SAG-Sep, a learning-based RCC separator built on a Sparse Augmented Graph representation of LP solutions that adds flow-informed long-range structure to the LP-support graph and enables single-pass neural separation. Exact separation revealed that high-value RCCs are often nested, onion-like, and the encoder reflects this in its scores; Onion-Sweep exploits it as a training-free decoder that tightens root bounds without extra forward passes or threshold tuning.
Beyond RCC separation, our results suggest that cut generation can be reframed as structured prediction over solver states, with attention architectures taking over expensive separation subroutines at the root node. Two directions follow: a learned decoder that reasons over candidate diversity and relaxation improvement could replace the hand-designed test-time logic, and reinforcement learning or online feedback could optimize entire cut sets rather than individual supervised labels.
