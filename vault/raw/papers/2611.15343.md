Abstract
Solving large-scale Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problems with Time
Windows (CVRPTW) remains a critical yet computationally intractable
challenge in real-world logistics systems, where strict operational
constraints and scale limit the effectiveness of both exact and
heuristic methods. At the same time, emerging quantum and
quantum-inspired approaches promise new optimization capabilities, but
their applicability to industrial-scale routing remains unclear.
This paper presents a hybrid optimization architecture that combines
classical Mixed Integer Linear Programming (MILP) for global
feasibility using Gurobi, with vehicle-level Quadratic Unconstrained Binary
Optimization (QUBO) subproblems solved using quantum-inspired Simulated
Bifurcation Machines (Toshiba SQBM+) and the Quantum Approximate
Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) on Amazon Braket backends (Rigetti
Ankaa-3 and IQM Emerald). The design is motivated by a
systematic analysis showing that monolithic CVRPTW QUBO formulations
are intractable even for small instances due to variable explosion and
unstable constraint enforcement.
The framework is evaluated on five realistic routing benchmarks derived
from real London traffic and location data (10–70 customers) with a
strict validation pipeline enforcing feasibility and monotone
improvement over classical baselines. SQBM+ achieves verified
fleet-distance reductions of

position hybrid classical–quantum architectures as a practical
near-term pathway for logistics optimization, while establishing
realistic performance boundaries for emerging quantum systems.

Introduction
The Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem with Time Windows (CVRPTW)
requires routing a fleet of vehicles to serve geographically distributed
customers under simultaneous capacity and per-customer service-time-window
constraints. It is NP-hard in the general
case
[
1
]
. As the number of customers and vehicles
grows, the number of feasible route-assignment combinations multiplies
rapidly: each additional delivery point expands both the routing search
space and the constraint enforcement burden, causing solver complexity
to escalate super-linearly (Fig.
1
). At real-world
scale with tens of delivery points, multiple vehicles, and tight operational
time windows, exact solvers exhaust their time budgets before finding
a provably good solution, while pure heuristics leave measurable
distance savings unrealized.
Quantum and quantum-inspired computing offer potential speedup for
combinatorial optimization via Quadratic Unconstrained Binary
Optimization (QUBO) models
[
4
,
5
]
. The
Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA)
[
2
]
and Simulated Bifurcation Machines (SBM)
[
3
,
8
]
are two leading approaches. SBM is quantum-inspired in the specific
sense that it descends from bifurcation-based adiabatic quantum
computation using coupled nonlinear oscillators, but replaces direct
quantum evolution by an efficiently simulated classical nonlinear
Hamiltonian dynamics. This makes it especially attractive for dense
QUBO/Ising subproblems because all variables can be updated
simultaneously and the dominant computation is a matrix-vector
interaction over the coupling matrix
[
8
,
10
]
.
Prior work
[
6
,
7
]
has formulated small VRP instances as QUBOs, but monolithic encodings of full CVRPTW remain intractable at practical scale: qubit counts,
penalty-coefficient stability, and time-window feasibility enforcement
all degrade rapidly with instance size (see Supplementary Information).
We address this gap with a three-stage
A
ssignment-
R
outing-
T
ime-check
(A-R-T) hybrid pipeline in which quantum and quantum-inspired solvers
handle only small per-vehicle subproblems, well within their tractable
range. Classical MILP (solved by Gurobi
[
11
]
) anchors every stage: it provides
complete end-to-end routing for smaller instances and assignment-only
solutions for larger ones, with quantum and quantum-inspired solvers
contributing route-sequence refinement as an additive polishing step.
As instance size grows, the pipeline transitions naturally from a
single-solver classical approach to an orchestrated multi-stage
architecture, leveraging each solver where it is most effective. The
framework is evaluated end-to-end on real London traffic data across
five instances (10 to 70 customers) and, to our knowledge, represents
the first multi-backend empirical study of quantum and quantum-inspired
methods on real-world CVRPTW instances at this scale.
