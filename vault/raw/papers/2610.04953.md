Abstract
Euclidean combinatorial optimization problems (ECOPs), such as the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP) and Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP), possess inherent symmetries under the two-dimensional Euclidean group E(2), including rotations, reflections, and translations. Existing learning-based methods, including recent diffusion-based methods, rely on data augmentation or regularization to approximate E(2)-equivariance. This paper presents EDISCO, the first discrete diffusion model for ECOPs with exact E(2)-invariant generative distributions over node-index solutions. EDISCO introduces an E(2)-equivariant edge-score network coupled with a categorical continuous-time Markov chain over discrete edge variables, and exact posterior sampling provides efficient multi-step inference. This design gives EDISCO a local geometric inductive bias: edge neighborhoods with the same relative geometry and combinatorial context are represented consistently regardless of absolute position or orientation, making learning more efficient and inference more robust than non-equivariant methods. EDISCO outperforms previous learning-based state-of-the-art solvers on synthetic TSP from 100 to 10000 nodes and CVRP from 50 to 2000 customers, while using only 33–50% of the training instances. Trained only on uniform synthetic data, EDISCO also outperforms competing learning-based baselines under spatial distribution shift and CVRP constraint-tightness shift. Code is available at
https://github.com/ValleyC/EDISCO
.

Introduction
Euclidean combinatorial optimization problems (ECOPs), such as the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP) and Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP), are core benchmarks for combinatorial optimization (CO) over spatial data. Classical solvers like Concorde and the LKH heuristic have been refined for decades
(
Applegate et al., 2006
;
Helsgaun, 2017
)
, while learning-based solvers have emerged as fast approximate alternatives with amortized inference and instance-level generalization
(
Kool et al., 2019
;
Joshi et al., 2022
)
. Diffusion-based methods now lead the non-autoregressive family, pushing optimality gaps below one percent on synthetic benchmarks and scaling ECOP solving from hundreds to ten thousand nodes
(
Sun and Yang, 2023
;
Li et al., 2023
;
Zhao et al., 2025
;
Li et al., 2024
;
Wang et al., 2025
;
Li et al., 2025b
;
Wang et al., 2026
;
Ma et al., 2025
)
. The field is now moving beyond chasing the last fraction of a percent on uniform synthetic benchmarks. Practical scenarios require solvers to remain reliable when the spatial distribution, problem size, or constraints shift away from training
(
Bi et al., 2022
;
Luo et al., 2025
;
Bi et al., 2026
)
, and robustness to such shifts is now a central criterion for whether learned solvers transfer beyond synthetic benchmarks.
A key obstacle is that ECOP solvers must learn reusable Euclidean structures, such as local distances, neighborhoods, edge crossings, depot-relative geometry, and capacity-pressure patterns. If a model recognizes these structures only in a particular coordinate frame, it must relearn the same patterns across rotated, reflected, and translated copies. The two-dimensional Euclidean group E(2) captures exactly these irrelevant coordinate dimensions. Rotations, reflections, and translations change the coordinate representation but not the node-index solution
(
Ouyang et al., 2024
;
Bronstein et al., 2021
)
. A reliable ECOP model should therefore learn geometric patterns that are constant on E(2) orbits, so the same local pattern induces the same decision wherever and however it appears in the plane. Existing neural CO methods approximate this principle through augmentation or symmetry regularization
(
Kim et al., 2022
)
. However, these mechanisms do not enforce exact equivariance or invariance by construction
(
Esteves et al., 2018
;
Nordenfors et al., 2025
;
Brehmer et al., 2025
)
. A diffusion model defines a generative distribution over solutions rather than a single deterministic prediction. Therefore, for diffusion-based CO, E(2) invariance must hold at the level of the entire distribution rather than only at the predicted output. Achieving this requires every stage of the pipeline (the diffusion model, reverse sampler, and decoder) to preserve the same symmetry. Existing diffusion solvers improve denoising quality, inference speed, local search, and expansion, but leave E(2) symmetry underexplored and provide no exact distribution-level invariance guarantee.
This work proposes EDISCO, the first discrete diffusion model for ECOPs whose generative distribution over node-index solutions is end-to-end E(2)-invariant. EDISCO introduces an E(2)-equivariant edge-score network coupled with a categorical continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC)
(
Campbell et al., 2022
)
over discrete edge variables, so the reverse diffusion process preserves geometric symmetry at the distribution level. Exact CTMC posterior sampling adapts multi-step solvers to discrete combinatorial variables, giving a controllable quality–speed trade-off without retraining. To ensure that the final feasibility step does not break the guarantee, EDISCO uses an invariant edge-expansion decoder that reads only edge probabilities, pairwise distances, and combinatorial feasibility constraints. The resulting model represents local edge neighborhoods with the same relative geometry consistently across positions and orientations, which makes learning more efficient and inference more robust than non-equivariant methods.
The contributions are as follows.
1.
Exact distribution-level E(2) invariance for generative ECOP solving.
EDISCO realizes orbit-shared local geometric structure by construction: scalar edge and message channels are constant on E(2) orbits, so equivalent local edge neighborhoods are represented consistently under E(2) transformations. The resulting generative distribution over node-index solutions is exactly E(2)-invariant. Proposition
1
formalizes this induced quotient-space reduction.
2.
Efficient continuous-time categorical diffusion for edge-valued CO.
EDISCO formulates edge selection as a categorical CTMC and uses exact posterior sampling to adapt multi-step solvers to discrete combinatorial variables. This provides flexible quality–speed trade-offs without retraining.
3.
Robust symmetry-preserving ECOP solving under spatial and constraint shifts.
The same invariance principle is preserved under sparse graph construction and capacity-conditioned CVRP features. As a result, EDISCO transfers from uniform synthetic training to spatial out-of-distribution (OOD) benchmarks and capacity-shifted CVRP while maintaining the same geometric symmetry guarantee.
EDISCO achieves state-of-the-art optimality gaps on TSP from 100 to 10000 nodes and on CVRP from 50 to 2000 customers, while using only 33% to 50% of the training instances compared to competing methods. With the model only trained on synthetic data, EDISCO outperforms non-equivariant baselines under spatial distribution shift
(
Bi et al., 2022
)
and remains state-of-the-art on real-world TSPLIB instances
(
Reinelt, 1991
)
. On CVRP, the same invariant-feature principle supports capacity-conditioned routing under the protocol of
Luo et al. (2025)
, where a single pre-trained model spans a range of capacity ratios without coordinate-frame dependence. Additional experiments on the Euclidean Steiner Tree Problem (ESTP) and the Maximum Independent Set (MIS) demonstrate EDISCO’s generality.

Discussion and Conclusion
We presented EDISCO, a discrete diffusion solver for Euclidean combinatorial optimization with an E(2)-invariant distribution over node-index solutions. The guarantee covers edge-score prediction, categorical reverse sampling, sparse graph construction, and feasibility-preserving decoding. Exact CTMC posterior sampling supports a flexible quality–speed trade-off without retraining. Across TSP and CVRP benchmarks, EDISCO achieves strong solution quality using fewer training instances and remains robust under spatial-distribution and capacity shifts.
The geometric guarantee applies to Euclidean problems such as TSP, CVRP, and ESTP. For arbitrary-graph problems such as MIS and Max-Cut, the optimum depends on topology rather than node coordinates, so the E(2)-specific contributions do not apply. The MIS experiment in Appendix
A.3
evaluates the categorical CTMC engine with a non-equivariant GNN. Asymmetric routing and real-world non-Euclidean network costs also lie outside the present geometric guarantee.
These limitations motivate future extensions through directional encoders, as in MatNet
(
Kwon et al., 2021
)
and GREAT
(
Lischka et al., 2025
)
, or coordinate representations of asymmetric costs learned through Finsler multi-dimensional scaling
(
Dagès et al., 2025
)
and related metric-learning methods. Studying equivariance in these representations and closing the sim-to-real gap remain future work. Within the Euclidean setting, EDISCO combines an exact geometric guarantee with the solution quality, data efficiency, and robustness demonstrated in our experiments.

Extended Related Work
Foundational neural combinatorial optimization.
Pointer Networks
[
Vinyals et al., 2015
]
introduced sequence-to-sequence learning for combinatorial structures, initially requiring supervised labels from exact solvers. RL approaches later removed the need for labeled optimal tours
[
Bello et al., 2016

Kool et al., 2019

and POMO

established strong autoregressive baselines for TSP and CVRP. These models demonstrated that neural policies can learn useful construction heuristics, but generalization to larger or shifted instances remains challenging
[
Fu et al., 2021
,
Joshi et al., 2022
]
.
Scaling beyond standard routing benchmarks.
Large-scale neural routing has motivated architectures that reduce the cost of encoding, decoding, or search. LEHD
[
Luo et al., 2023
]
separates lightweight encoding from heavier decoding to improve large-scale generalization. BQ-NCO
[
Drakulic et al., 2023
]
uses bisimulation quotienting for stronger zero-shot generalization. GLOP
[
Ye et al., 2024
]
and UDC

use divide-and-conquer strategies for large CVRP, while hierarchical real-world TSP solvers operate across multiple spatial resolutions

Goh et al., 2024
]
. UniCO
[
Pan et al., 2025
]
and GOAL
[
Drakulic et al., 2025
]
pursue broader problem unification. These works show that practical neural CO increasingly depends on scale, robustness, and transfer rather than only synthetic benchmark gaps.
Discrete diffusion and flow-based generative models.
Discrete diffusion models provide a natural substrate for categorical structures
[
Austin et al., 2021
]
. Related generative modeling frameworks include continuous diffusion for categorical data
[
Dieleman et al., 2022
]
, variational flow matching
[
Eijkelboom et al., 2024

, discrete flow matching
[
Gat et al., 2024

[
Lipman et al., 2023

[
Qin et al., 2025

demonstrates the strength of discrete flow matching. These developments are complementary to EDISCO: they improve generative modeling machinery, while EDISCO studies exact Euclidean symmetry for edge-valued combinatorial diffusion.
Gradient-based discrete sampling.
EDLP
[
Mohanty et al., 2025
]
uses a Gaussian coupling between discrete states and a continuous auxiliary variable, whose local-entropy guidance biases discrete Langevin proposals toward flat modes. HiSS
[
Mohanty and Zhang, 2026
]
uses logistic bridging in a Metropolis-within-Gibbs scheme to move between separated modes, followed by local gradient-based discrete refinement. These proposals “see the landscape” through the flatness and barriers of a specified distribution over candidate solutions. For TSP, this is an instance’s tour-cost landscape. EDISCO learns relative city-layout patterns from solution labels and reuses the same trained conditional model across instances, preserving E(2)-invariance through categorical edge diffusion and feasibility decoding.
Theory, alignment, and data efficiency.
The expressiveness of GNNs is limited by their aggregation structure
[
Xu et al., 2019
]
, and algorithmic alignment argues that architectures generalize better when their computations match the target algorithmic structure
[
Xu et al., 2020
]
. For Euclidean CO, equivariance supplies such alignment by quotienting out coordinate-frame transformations. An empirical scaling study finds improved data and compute efficiency from equivariance, while augmentation can close the data-efficiency gap given sufficient training
[
Brehmer et al., 2025
]
. Work on TSP generalization also identifies limits of purely supervised learning
[
Joshi et al., 2022
]
. These results motivate EDISCO’s use of exact distribution-level E(2) invariance rather than augmentation-only symmetry learning.
Hybrid neural-classical optimization.
Many practical solvers combine learned predictions with classical optimization. Neural local rewriting learns to improve existing solutions through local modifications
[
Chen and Tian, 2019
]
; learned branching and neural diving accelerate mixed-integer programming
[
Gasse et al., 2019

Nair et al., 2020

Pirnay and Grimm, 2024
