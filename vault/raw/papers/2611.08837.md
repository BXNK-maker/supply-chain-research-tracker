Abstract
Equivalent encodings of an optimization problem can produce different learned predictions. Interpreting these differences requires accounting for numerical error and multiple optima. We construct metamorphic tests for optimization solver surrogates with explicit eligibility conditions, output alignment, and numerical thresholds. Reference-solver agreement and independent constraint checks determine which sampled pairs enter the audit. We study alternating-current optimal power flow (AC-OPF), the traveling salesman problem (TSP), the capacitated vehicle routing problem (CVRP), and maximum independent set (MIS). Across five grids, GridSFM-Open violates each of 13 relations on at least one grid, and 12 by more than twice the threshold. Randomly initialized copies of the grid models fail nearly the same relations, so the failures do not require training. Source patches and input canonicalization remove selected discrepancies, but no intervention establishes preserved accuracy and feasibility within the declared margin on every grid. In routing, Policy Optimization with Multiple Optima (POMO) has a single-pass rotation/reflection violation rate that rises from 12.7% to 89.5% between 20 and 100 cities. Graph-Based Diffusion Solvers for Combinatorial Optimization (DIFUSCO) violates the MIS disjoint-union relation on 35.6% of draws. Post hoc normalization within connected components removes these violations on the tested graphs. These findings support reporting metamorphic consistency alongside optimality, feasibility, and accuracy, and validating repair quality separately from consistency.

A complementary check asks whether predictions depend on how the same problem is encoded. Renumbering buses, i.e., the grid’s connection points, should only renumber predicted settings, while reordering line-table rows should leave them unchanged. Ordinary accuracy and feasibility checks do not directly test this internal consistency. Yet renumbering the standard 14-bus grid (IEEE case14) changes GridSFM-Open’s reactive-power prediction by 0.30 per unit (p.u.), or 30 megavolt-amperes reactive (MVAr), about 30,000 times our threshold. Per-unit values express quantities relative to their declared bases. Reordering line-table rows changes the prediction by 0.60 p.u. III-B
Generation Rule and Evidentiary Tiers
We construct relations by tracing how stored fields enter
P
P
. Ignored fields suggest inert edits, while labels, row order, and orientation suggest alternative encodings. Aggregates allow component splitting that preserves their total effect. Padding and insertion require exact reductions, and independent instances allow disjoint union. Each candidate needs eligibility conditions, an output pull-back, an objective offset, and a tier describing the kind of preservation claimed.
Tier A
preserves the physical problem through numbering, row order, orientation, reference choice, or Euclidean rotation/reflection.
Tier B
changes structure through splitting, padding, union, or insertion. Its pull-back may be many-to-one, but every transformed optimum must reduce to an original optimum.
Tier C
rescales inputs, potentially moving them outside the training distribution or losing equivalence under rounding. We exclude it from the main findings.
Tier D
edits inert fields or objective constants without changing optimal solutions. We verify inertness on the problem actually constructed by the solver interface (Section
VI-D
). Tiers describe the relation and its justification, not the severity of a violation.

IX
Threats to Validity
Construct validity
Calibration is empirical and supplies no universal round-off bound. Without precision controls, we interpret the float32-only releases’ discrepancies by magnitude. Tier assignment also requires judgment about the applicable reduction. For AC-OPF, admission establishes agreement between local solves. Neither multistart spread nor unvalidated QC extrema certify the range of global optima. Recovering one objective cluster cannot exclude undiscovered optima, and bounds on global optima do not apply to costlier local solutions.
Internal validity
The interventions support causal explanations on tested inputs, but canonical GridSFM and LUMINA, fine-tuned GridSFM, and DIFUSCO component normalization were developed post hoc. Mechanism detection uses disjoint sources to limit reuse of discovery examples, yet covers only discovered mechanisms, one seed stream, and no case2000 run. Without multiple-comparison control, quality tests give individual confidence statements rather than simultaneous guarantees.
Reliability
Screens use macOS arm64, fine-tuning uses a graphics processor, and initial QC computations use Linux x86-64. IPOPT’s cross-platform controls agree, but Linux replay changes above-floor magnitudes by up to 53%. It preserves all tested OPF verdicts and all TSP verdicts except one float32 aug

External validity
The selected subjects and grids establish counterexamples rather than failure prevalence among learned solvers. Training coverage differs: all five grids occur in LUMINA’s training card, while case14 and case118 lie outside GridSFM’s stated range. We have not compared the protocol’s cost or effectiveness with ordinary accuracy or residual checks, so its relative efficiency remains unknown.
X
Conclusion
Solver-admitted metamorphic testing exposes dependence on input representations that ordinary prediction metrics do not directly measure. The grid and MIS investigations locate failures in index use, row positions, stored directions, and normalization across disconnected components. Random-weight controls show that failures can occur before training, while routing results show that frequent violations can coexist with small optimality gaps. Relabeling or symmetry tests alone leave other failures undetected, so testing should cover both alternative encodings and structural reductions.
Diagnosis and repair require distinct evidence. Admission checks the sampled transformation, calibration assesses numerical variation, and aligned comparisons expose inconsistency. None alone guarantees correctness. Canonicalization can remove a discrepancy while leaving the encoding-sensitive operation in place. No tested grid intervention establishes accuracy and feasibility non-inferiority everywhere, and the minimum relation sets cover only the discovered mechanisms. Applying the method to new solvers requires problem-specific reductions, an explicit account of certificate strength, and alignment of random choices for corresponding variables. Future work should validate bounds on the set of optima, test composed relations and unexplained structural failures, and assess whether training from scratch on repaired features preserves consistency and solution quality.
