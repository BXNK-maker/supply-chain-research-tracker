Abstract
The paper presents an Industry Process for Meta-heuristic Development (IPMD) schema, a systematic methodology for applying meta- and hyper-heuristic applications to optimization problems. In this context, we present a case study of Balancing Non-dominated Tournament Genetic Programming (BNTGP) applied to solve two NP-hard multi-objective optimization problems: routing (Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem with Time Windows) and scheduling (Multi-Skill Resource Constrained Project Scheduling Problem). The BNTGP method is based on a metaheuristic design. Its genetic programming-based representation makes BNTGP a hyper-heuristic, as it enables the exploration of a search space of heuristics for solving problems in general. The resulting heuristic can then be applied across a set of problem instances.

Introduction
The process of applying meta- and hyper-heuristics optimization to different complex problems requires multiple steps. First, researchers need to deeply understand the problem structure, constraints, choose benchmark instances, and propose a problem representation. Then they need to select, adjust, and tune the optimization method, and analyze its performance relative to other methods.
According to the No Free Lunch (NFL) Theorem, the performance of all metaheuristic search algorithms is averaged across all possible optimization problems. However, incorporating domain knowledge can make metaheuristic design more effective in problem-aware optimization processes. Such a strategy requires experts to analyze the given problem, its instances, and the solution landscape, to define problem-specific heuristics, i.e., a solution representation, and crossover/mutation/selection operators. A hyper-heuristic can address some of these challenges by automatically adapting to different problems and scenarios, which is particularly useful for novel problems and practical applications.
This paper presents the methodology for applying the novel multi-objective Balancing Non-dominated Tournament Genetic Programming (BNTGP)

This study discusses how domain knowledge is incorporated into the method and identifies common design principles that facilitate its application to new domains.

BNTGP for MS-RCPSP and CVRPTW: A Case Study
The
BNTGP
is a Hyper-Heuristic for multi-objective (MO) optimization. The method combines the state-of-the-art metaheuristic Balanced Non-Dominated Tournament Genetic Algorithm (BNTGA)
[
1
]
and Genetic Programming (GP). The construction of an individual is performed by a heuristic decoder that iteratively selects the best feasible candidate action at each construction step. To determine which action is best, the method extracts features for each candidate action and combines them into a single score using an expression. Each expression is represented in the form of a tree, where the input feature values are leaves, nodes represent mathematical operations, and the root of the tree outputs a score. During optimization, a population of trees is evolved using genetic programming, where each tree is used to construct a single solution. The usage of GP allows BNTGP to automatically adjust to different problem instances. Additionally, it supports feature selection, as the features that do not contribute to the quality of results are not preserved during the evolution of trees.

Conclusions and future works
In this paper, the BNTGP hyper-heuristic is briefly presented in the context of IPMD as a framework for development of metaheuristics for real-world applications. Despite substantial differences between the two problems, the application of BNTGP followed a similar general pattern. The evolutionary framework remained unchanged, while domain knowledge was introduced through the design of candidate action features and decoder logic. Future work will investigate additional ways of incorporating domain knowledge into BNTGP, including automatic feature design and problem-specific feature selection. Another important direction is to study the transferability and interpretability of evolved heuristics across different instances and optimization problems.
