Abstract
Short-term bike-sharing demand forecasting is complicated by spatial-temporal non-stationarity and the practical difficulty of incorporating unstructured external text into numerical pipelines. Conventional approaches rely on historical flow sequences and fixed graph structures, thereby constraining their accuracy when anomalous social events perturb normal travel patterns. We propose a forecasting framework in which a Large Language Model (LLM) drives a semantic shockwave mechanism that converts free-form urban text—municipal event schedules, local news, and transit bulletins—into quantified spatial-temporal perturbation fields. The LLM extracts three physically interpretable parameters per event (intensity, spatial reach, and temporal lag), from which Gaussian decay fields are constructed and injected into a Zero-Inflated Adaptive Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ZI-ASTGCN). To handle the pronounced sparsity of grid-level measurements, the model couples a dual-branch output head with a multi-task zero-inflated loss that jointly trains a gating probability and a conditional flow intensity. Experiments on the operational Barcelona Bicing dataset show that ZI-ASTGCN outperforms established neural baselines, with particularly strong gains during high-demand periods, validating the utility of physics-grounded semantic signals in spatial-temporal mobility forecasting.

State-of-the-art spatial-temporal models—including graph convolutional and attention-based architectures—are fundamentally closed numerical systems

The consequence is that demand surges driven by large-scale events appear indistinguishable from random noise until their effects are already visible in the observation stream

Recent work on Large Language Models (LLMs) has demonstrated their capacity to distill unstructured text into features compatible with time-series models
[
14

15

. Several studies tokenize multi-modal traffic sequences for transformer-based encoding
[
12
,
16
]
, while others align text-derived embeddings directly with sensor observations
[
13
,
17

. A common limitation of these approaches is that they encode semantic information as static latent vectors, without modeling how an event’s influence spreads outward from its origin and attenuates over distance and time. For instance, the influence of a stadium concert does not instantaneously materialize across the city; it propagates gradually and decays with spatial separation and temporal distance from the event peak.
We address this gap by proposing a framework that models urban events as parameterized physical impulses. A reasoning-focused LLM parses each narrative text entry into three scalar parameters—event intensity, spatial impact radius, and temporal lag—which are then used to construct continuous Gaussian decay fields on an hourly spatial grid. These semantic shockwave fields serve as conditioning signals for a Zero-Inflated Adaptive Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ZI-ASTGCN), which extends the baseline ASTGCN
[
18
]
with a cross-attention fusion layer and a dual-branch prediction head. The dual-branch design simultaneously models the probability of demand occurrence (via a learned gating mechanism) and the conditional demand magnitude (via a Huber-loss regressor), directly addressing the zero-inflated and heavy-tailed nature of fine-grained mobility observations.
The main contributions of this study are:
•
We propose a semantic shockwave framework that derives explicit physical parameters from unstructured urban text using an LLM, transforming narrative event descriptions into continuous spatio-temporal Gaussian decay fields that integrate seamlessly with graph convolutional architectures.
•
We introduce ZI-ASTGCN, which incorporates these semantic fields using a cross-attention fusion module and a dual-branch output layer trained with a multi-task zero-inflated objective, enabling a clear disentanglement between demand occurrence and demand intensity in sparse, highly skewed data.
•
We conduct an extensive empirical study on the real-world Barcelona Bicing dataset, including benchmarks against spatio-temporal neural baselines, an internal ablation focused on the LLM component, and a dedicated event-period error metric (EvtRMSE) designed to isolate model accuracy during active semantic shockwave intervals.

II-B

Standard embedding models, like BERT and sentence transformers, focus on lexical proximity but lack guidance on how urban disturbances spread spatially and decay over time. The semantic shockwave mechanism improves upon this by treating each textual event as a discrete impulse that spreads through a spatial grid using a Gaussian decay function.

III
Experiments
III-A
Data Acquisition and Description
The evaluation dataset covers the period from April 1 to June 30, 2025, in Barcelona, and includes normal commuting patterns as well as major urban events, thus providing a challenging testbed for anomaly-driven demand prediction.
III-A
1
Bike-sharing system flow
Operational records are drawn from 510 active Bicing docking stations at 5-minute resolution. Rather than reconstructing origin-destination trip trajectories—which are incomplete and license-restricted—we infer demand from signed temporal differences in dock occupancy. Positive differentials correspond to departures (outflow) and negative differentials to arrivals (inflow).

Static spatial characteristics are extracted from OpenStreetMap (OSM): approximately 79,000 POIs classified into 14 functional categories, including transport nodes, retail hubs, and educational facilities. One caveat is that STGCN attains a slightly better outflow Tail-RMSE (7.469) than the full model (7.679); this isolated exception may arise from STGCN’s spectral filters aligning with a specific frequency band of outflow spikes and merits additional analysis.

IV-B
Event-Driven Demand Anticipation
Figure
7

The plotted windows are centered on intervals of active semantic shockwaves (shaded in orange), each corresponding to a large scheduled event in the input corpus.
During these windows, observed demand (solid black) rises sharply and departs from the periodic baseline. Autoregressive models are inherently reactive: they begin to adjust only after the anomaly enters the input buffer, accumulating a phase delay proportional to the look-back window length. ZI-ASTGCN, informed by the shockwave fields ahead of time, tracks both the onset and the peak amplitude of each surge with substantially lower event-period RMSE. The contrast is most pronounced at Plaça Catalunya, where foot traffic spillover from large concerts is not spatially proximate to any single transit terminal, making it poorly handled by topology-only models.

Spatial Error Distribution and Robustness
Figure

Prediction errors peak near Sants Station and the central commercial corridor, where absolute traffic volumes are highest—an expected outcome given that RMSE grows proportionally with flow magnitude. Without this adaptive rewiring, prediction errors would propagate laterally through the graph, inflating RMSE in cells that share a structural edge with a surge node but are not themselves affected by the event. V
Conclusion and Future Work
This study presented an LLM-augmented spatio-temporal framework for grid-level bike-sharing demand forecasting to address the limited capability of conventional graph-based models in capturing demand disturbances induced by urban events. The proposed semantic shockwave mechanism converts textual event descriptions into parameterized Gaussian decay fields and integrates them into a ZI-ASTGCN through cross-attention and a dual-branch zero-inflated output layer, enabling the model to jointly account for sparse observations and event-driven demand surges within a unified architecture. Experiments on the Barcelona Bicing dataset showed consistent improvements over representative spatio-temporal neural baselines, with larger gains observed during event periods, while ablation analysis indicated that semantic event representations contributed measurable performance improvements beyond the graph structure alone. These results suggest that incorporating structured, physically motivated semantic features can improve event-aware spatio-temporal forecasting. Nevertheless, several limitations remain. The current LLM-based parameter extraction is conducted offline and may limit deployment in real-time applications; additional data sources, such as video streams and social media signals, were not considered and may improve the detection of unscheduled events; and the generalizability of the proposed shockwave representation across cities with different urban forms and bike-sharing systems requires further validation
